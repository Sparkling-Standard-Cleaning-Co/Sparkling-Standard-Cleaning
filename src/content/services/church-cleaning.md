## Cleaning that respects the space

A sanctuary isn't just a room with chairs. The way it's cleaned — carefully, quietly, with respect for what happens there — matters as much as the result. The founder's faith is personal, and serving churches is something this company genuinely wants to do well.

That means realistic scheduling around services and events, attention to the rooms that guests notice first (entryways, restrooms, nurseries), and detail work that keeps up with a building that hosts people all week long.

## A scope built around your congregation

Every church is different: a small sanctuary and fellowship hall, a campus with classrooms and offices, or a historic building with surfaces that need a careful hand. The walkthrough step exists to understand your building — what gets used hardest, what needs the most attention, and when the building is actually available to clean.

After the walkthrough you'll receive a clear scope and quote: what's cleaned, how often, around which services, and the price. There's no instant online pricing for church facilities because an honest number requires seeing the building.

## Scheduling around church life

Weekday cleaning between services is the usual rhythm; larger preparation cleanings can be scheduled before Easter, Christmas and community events. Evening and early-morning windows are available when the building's calendar requires them. Tell us how your building operates — the schedule is built to fit it, not the other way around.

## Volunteers deserve backup

Many churches depend on volunteer cleaning teams who serve faithfully for years. Hiring help isn't a rejection of that service — it's a way to give those volunteers their time back while keeping the building held to a standard the congregation can be proud of.
