# Terms and policy research memorandum

**Prepared:** 2026-10-27
**Status:** Internal research record; not legal advice. Public terms were updated from this review;
the owner-approved fee schedule and Florida-licensed attorney review remain open gates.

## Why this review exists

Initiative 6.8 asks for stronger, commercially reasonable terms that protect Sparkling Standard
LLC's time, scheduling capacity, travel, labor, equipment, reputation and assets without inventing
penalties that a court would treat as unenforceable. The public terms therefore separate:

1. rules that are currently operational and enforceable;
2. statutory rights that cannot be contracted away;
3. proposed fees/terms that remain disabled until the owner approves a written schedule and a
   qualified attorney reviews them.

## Decisions reflected in the published terms

- **Request versus booking:** a request or temporary hold is not a confirmed appointment; the owner
  confirms scope, date, arrival window and price.
- **Cancellation:** existing owner configuration requires 24 hours' notice. No standard fee is
  currently enabled. The three-business-day statutory cancellation right is disclosed separately
  and takes priority.
- **Material condition mismatch:** the first remedy is an on-site reassessment before work, then a
  revised written quote, scope adjustment, reschedule or refusal. No "resubmittal fee" is published
  or collected. An honest mistake is not treated as fraud.
- **Failed access:** document the visit and discuss reasonable documented travel/labor costs; no
  automatic fee is currently collected.
- **Damage:** the company remains responsible for its own negligence. Pre-existing damage, ordinary
  wear, customer-supplied products and inaccessible conditions are excluded. Claims should be
  reported promptly (seven days requested) and investigated.
- **Recurring service:** the initial Deep Cleaning remains mandatory; no silent recurring card
  charges; the three-business-day cancellation notice applies to continuing service contracts.
- **Gift certificates:** no expiration, no post-sale fees, one-time redemption, balance tracking,
  and no promise that a certificate covers a whole cleaning.
- **Disputes:** Florida law and Escambia County venue, subject to non-waivable consumer rights; no
  arbitration clause or class-action waiver.

## Proposed owner approval gates (not active)

No new monetary penalty is published or enforced. The following are recommendations for the owner
and counsel to decide:

1. **Late cancellation / failed access:** a fee equal to documented round-trip travel plus a
   reasonable preparation allowance, capped at a stated maximum and disclosed before acceptance.
   Avoid arbitrary percentages or punitive amounts.
2. **Material scope discrepancy:** no separate fee by default. Use a revised written quote or
   reschedule. If the customer declines after a documented wasted visit, the same approved
   late-cancellation formula could apply.
3. **Recurring agreements:** a written term/termination schedule, including the statutory
   three-business-day notice and any owner-approved late-cancellation terms.
4. **Gift certificates:** owner-approved denominations, refund/cash-out treatment, delivery method,
   redemption ledger and unclaimed-property handling. Sales remain off until these are approved.
5. **Commercial/church:** written proposals should contain service scope, access, invoicing,
   termination, additional-work authorization and church-discount eligibility.

## Remaining legal uncertainties

- Whether every recurring cleaning arrangement is a "contract for future consumer services ...
  on a continuing basis" under Rule 2-18.002; the site applies the three-business-day notice
  conservatively to recurring arrangements.
- Whether a Florida choice-of-law/venue clause is enforceable against an Alabama consumer in a
  particular claim.
- Whether a future cancellation/access fee will be treated as liquidated damages or a penalty based
  on actual proof of lost scheduling capacity.
- Whether any damage-liability limitation is enforceable for a particular claim; the public terms
  do not waive liability for the company's own negligence.
- Gift-certificate unclaimed-property treatment in Alabama and Florida, including any retail
  exemption, requires confirmation for the final business model.
- Attorney-fee, arbitration and class-action provisions were intentionally not added; whether to
  add any should be decided by Florida counsel, not by website copy.

## Required professional review

A Florida-licensed attorney should review the final public terms, the owner-approved fee schedule,
the gift-certificate program and any Alabama-specific consumer terms before new fees or gift sales
are activated. This memorandum is an engineering/compliance research record, not a substitute for
that review.
