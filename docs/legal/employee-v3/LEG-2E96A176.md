# Employee Handbook

## 1. Introduction and Application

1.1 This Employee Handbook describes the workplace expectations, payroll practices, safety rules, and employee benefits of Sparkling Standard Cleaning Co. It applies to all employees unless a written policy or a collective bargaining agreement states otherwise.

1.2 This Handbook is a statement of current policies. It is not an employment contract and does not create a fixed term, a guarantee of continued employment, or a contractual right to a benefit.

1.3 Employment is at-will to the extent permitted by Florida law. Either the employee or Sparkling Standard may end the employment relationship at any time, with or without cause or notice, subject to applicable law.

1.4 Sparkling Standard may change a policy, benefit, or practice in writing. A change applies prospectively unless the law requires otherwise.

1.5 The employee should ask the owner about any policy the employee does not understand.

## 2. Equal Employment Opportunity

2.1 Sparkling Standard provides equal employment opportunity without regard to race, color, religion, sex, pregnancy, national origin, age, disability, genetic information, marital status, military or veteran status, or any other characteristic protected by law.

2.2 Employment decisions are based on lawful, job-related qualifications, reliability, conduct, performance, and operational needs.

2.3 Sparkling Standard will provide a reasonable accommodation for a known disability or a sincerely held religious practice when the accommodation is required by law and does not create an undue hardship.

2.4 An employee who needs an accommodation should contact the owner. Medical records and diagnosis details are not required for an initial request.

## 3. Workplace Conduct and Professional Standards

3.1 Sparkling Standard expects employees to work responsibly, treat customers and coworkers professionally, and represent the company appropriately.

3.2 Employees are free to hold and express personal beliefs, opinions, and viewpoints. Ordinary conversation, disagreement, humor, and lawful personal expression are not automatically workplace misconduct simply because someone disagrees or takes offense.

3.3 Employees must perform their duties, respect customer property and privacy, follow reasonable workplace instructions, and maintain professional conduct while representing the company.

3.4 Sparkling Standard does not permit unlawful discrimination, unlawful harassment, threats, violence, retaliation, or conduct that materially interferes with a safe and productive working environment.

3.5 Employees may raise a good-faith concern without retaliation, including through an alternative channel when the concern involves the owner or a supervisor.

3.6 Nothing in this policy restricts a right protected by applicable law.

## 4. Reporting Concerns and Nonretaliation

4.1 An employee should report a concern about discrimination, harassment, safety, wage payment, customer conduct, or a company practice to the owner or another appropriate person.

4.2 Sparkling Standard will review a good-faith report and take appropriate action. An employee who reports in good faith will not be retaliated against, even if the report is not substantiated.

4.3 An employee may communicate with a government agency, participate in an investigation, discuss wages or working conditions as protected by law, or exercise another protected right.

4.4 Retaliation for protected activity is prohibited.

## 5. Scheduling and Attendance

5.1 Sparkling Standard schedules cleaning work according to customer appointments, travel time, and operational needs. The employee will receive the assignment and arrival window as far in advance as practicable.

5.2 The employee must report on time and be ready to work at the assigned location. Repeated lateness, unexcused absence, or failure to communicate may lead to corrective action.

5.3 The employee must notify the owner or supervisor as soon as the employee knows that an assignment cannot be worked. The employee should not arrange a substitute without authorization.

5.4 The employee must not accept a side job from a customer during a Sparkling Standard assignment or use company time or supplies for personal work.

5.5 The employee will follow the customer-access instructions and will not enter a property without authorization.

## 6. Timekeeping and Compensable Time

6.1 The employee must record actual time worked using the approved timekeeping method. Recording time for another employee, altering another employee's record, or recording time not worked is prohibited.

6.2 Compensable time includes all time the employee is required to be at work, waiting for work, performing work, or traveling for the employer's benefit. The employee must not perform work off the clock.

6.3 The employee must clock in when work begins and clock out when work ends. If the employee performs work before or after the recorded time, the employee must record the actual time.

6.4 A rest break of the length required by law is compensable. A bona fide meal break during which the employee is completely relieved of duty is unpaid.

6.5 The employee must report any missed, incomplete, or incorrect time record promptly. Sparkling Standard will correct the record and pay the employee for all compensable time.

6.6 Sparkling Standard does not permit rounding that reduces compensable time or a practice that automatically deducts a break the employee did not take.

## 7. Travel Between Jobs

7.1 Travel from one job site to another during the workday is compensable working time when it is required for the employer's work. The employee must record it as travel and must not clock out to make work-related travel disappear.

7.2 The employee should use the approved travel action when leaving one job for the next. Travel directly between job sites is not an unpaid lunch break.

7.3 Ordinary home-to-work and work-to-home commuting is not compensable unless the employee is required to report to a company location, carry equipment, perform work during the commute, or the law otherwise requires payment.

7.4 The employee must follow the assigned route and report a material delay. Personal errands during compensated travel are not permitted.

## 8. Overtime

8.1 Sparkling Standard pays overtime as required by federal and Florida law. Work beyond the applicable overtime threshold must be authorized in advance when practicable.

8.2 The employee must record all hours worked. Overtime is paid whether or not it was preauthorized; unauthorized overtime may lead to corrective action, but the time is still paid.

8.3 The employee should tell the supervisor if an assignment is likely to require more time than scheduled.

## 9. Meals and Breaks

9.1 The employee should take a 30-minute unpaid meal break when the assignment permits and the employee is completely relieved of duty.

9.2 A meal break should be recorded accurately. If the employee works through a meal break or remains on duty, the time is compensable.

9.3 Rest breaks required by law are paid and should be recorded as required by the timekeeping system.

## 10. Pay and Payroll Corrections

10.1 Sparkling Standard pays wages on the regular payroll schedule. The employee should review each pay statement for hours, rate, overtime, reimbursements, and deductions.

10.2 An employee who believes a payment is incorrect should report it promptly to the owner or payroll provider. Sparkling Standard will investigate and correct a verified error.

10.3 The employee must not falsify a time record, expense, mileage report, or other payroll information.

10.4 Deductions are made only as required by law or authorized by the employee under a lawful written authorization.

## 11. Uniforms and Appearance

11.1 Sparkling Standard provides the approved uniform and identification. The employee will wear the uniform, keep it clean, and present a professional appearance.

11.2 The employee will not alter, deface, or add an unauthorized logo to the uniform.

11.3 Personal hygiene and safe work clothing are required. The employee should tell the owner if a uniform or safety item does not fit or is damaged.

## 12. Customer Privacy and Property

12.1 The employee will access a customer's property only for the assigned work and will not read, copy, photograph, or remove documents, mail, screens, prescriptions, family photographs, or private information.

12.2 The employee will not discuss a customer's identity, address, belongings, schedule, or personal information outside the assigned work.

12.3 The employee will treat customer property with care, follow the approved scope, and report damage or an incident promptly.

12.4 Photographs may be taken only for the approved quality or workflow purpose. The employee will not publish a customer photograph or post a property image on social media.

12.5 Residential physical house keys are not retained. Commercial keys or badges are handled only under a signed custody authorization.

## 13. Company Equipment and Supplies

13.1 The employee will use company equipment and supplies only for authorized work and will follow the instructions for each product.

13.2 The employee will report a damaged, lost, or missing item promptly. Equipment remains company property and must be returned at separation.

13.3 The employee will not take a company supply, tool, or customer item for personal use.

13.4 The employee will keep chemicals in labeled containers and will not mix products unless the label and training permit it.

## 14. Chemical Handling and Safety

14.1 The employee will follow the Safety Data Sheet, product label, and training for each chemical.

14.2 The employee will wear the required personal protective equipment, including gloves, eye protection, and a respirator when the product or condition requires it.

14.3 The employee will not mix bleach with ammonia, acid, or another incompatible product. The employee will not use an unlabeled container.

14.4 The employee will ventilate an area when required and will stop work if a chemical exposure, spill, or unsafe condition occurs.

14.5 The employee will report a spill, exposure, injury, or near miss immediately.

## 15. Personal Protective Equipment and Safe Work

15.1 The employee will use the PPE and safe-work methods provided for the task.

15.2 The employee will not climb on a counter, chair, or unstable surface. Ladder or height work is performed only when authorized and safe.

15.3 The employee will keep walkways clear, use caution with wet floors, and use proper lifting technique.

15.4 The employee will stop work and report a threat, weapon, hazardous condition, or unsafe situation.

## 16. Incident and Injury Reporting

16.1 The employee must report a work-related injury, illness, exposure, vehicle incident, property damage, or near miss as soon as practicable, and in any event by the end of the workday.

16.2 The employee will complete the required incident report and cooperate with the investigation and any workers' compensation process.

16.3 The employee should seek emergency medical care when needed. The report does not replace emergency care.

16.4 Retaliation for reporting an injury or safety concern is prohibited.

## 17. Vehicles and Travel

17.1 The employee will use a company vehicle only when authorized and will follow all traffic laws.

17.2 The employee will not use a phone while driving unless hands-free and permitted by law.

17.3 The employee will report an accident, citation, or vehicle defect promptly.

17.4 Personal vehicle use for company travel is reimbursed only under the approved mileage or travel policy.

## 18. Customer Access and Security

18.1 The employee will follow the access method in the signed authorization and will not accept a key concealed on the property.

18.2 The employee will not bypass an alarm, force a door, disable a camera, or remain in a property without authorization.

18.3 The employee will report a lost or compromised key, fob, badge, or code immediately.

18.4 The employee will not give a customer, family member, or another vendor access to a secured area without authorization.

## 19. Mobile Devices, Portal, and Communications

19.1 The employee may use a personal phone for scheduling and timekeeping as permitted by the owner. Personal use should not interfere with work or create a safety risk.

19.2 The employee will use the employee portal only for authorized work and will protect the employee's login credentials.

19.3 The employee will not share a password, session, or access code. A suspected compromise must be reported promptly.

19.4 The employee will communicate with customers only through the approved channel and will not make an unauthorized promise about price, scope, or a refund.

## 20. GPS and Timekeeping Disclosure

20.1 The employee portal or timekeeping application may capture location when the employee taps a clock or travel action. Location is used for timekeeping, dispatch, routing, safety, and service verification.

20.2 Sparkling Standard does not use the application to track off-duty activity. Location capture should occur only when the employee uses an authorized timekeeping action.

20.3 The employee should report a location or timekeeping error promptly so the record can be corrected.

## 21. Confidentiality and Company Information

21.1 The employee will protect nonpublic customer lists, contact records, pricing, account information, internal procedures, credentials, business plans, financial information, and private marketing or operational information.

21.2 The employee will use confidential information only for authorized company work and will not disclose it to an unauthorized person.

21.3 These obligations do not restrict lawful whistleblowing, communication with a government agency, protected wage or working-condition discussions, protected concerted activity, or another legally protected activity.

21.4 The employee will return company and customer information at separation and will not delete information subject to a legal or investigation hold.

21.5 The separate Employee Confidentiality and Client Privacy Agreement contains additional detail.

## 22. Discipline and Corrective Action

22.1 Sparkling Standard may use coaching, a written corrective-action record, a performance improvement plan, suspension, or termination as appropriate.

22.2 Corrective action is not required to follow a fixed sequence. Serious misconduct, including violence, theft, harassment, falsification of records, or a safety violation, may result in immediate action.

22.3 The employee will receive an opportunity to respond to a factual concern unless an immediate safety or legal concern requires otherwise.

22.4 A corrective-action record is not a contract and does not change the at-will relationship.

## 23. Separation and Return of Property

23.1 The employee must return all company property, uniforms, equipment, keys, fobs, badges, documents, and records at separation or when requested.

23.2 The employee must not retain customer information, access credentials, or company records.

23.3 The employee will cooperate with an exit review and the return of any customer property.

23.4 Final pay and any deduction are handled according to applicable law and the employee's written authorizations.

## 24. Acknowledgment

24.1 The employee acknowledges receipt of this Handbook and understands that the employee should ask questions about any policy.

24.2 The employee understands that this Handbook is not an employment contract and does not create a fixed term or a guarantee of continued employment.

24.3 The employee understands that the separate Confidentiality, Nonsolicitation, Noncompetition (if applicable), and Property/Credential Return Agreements may impose additional obligations.

24.4 The employee confirms that the employee can read and understand this Handbook or has received an accommodation.
