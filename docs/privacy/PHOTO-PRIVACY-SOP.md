# Photo & privacy SOP

Photos serve two purposes with completely different rules:

| Purpose | Audience | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Operational proof** (completion documentation, issue reports, checklist evidence) | The customer | Shared with the customer only. Privacy rules still apply. |
| **Marketing use** | The public | **Customer permission required**, plus every rule below. |

## Never photograph or publish

- Street numbers, house numbers, mail, packages with names
- Bills, bank statements, prescriptions, medical documents, tax papers
- Personal identification of any kind
- Family photographs where avoidable (ask customers to put away sentimental/family photos if a
  shot requires the area)
- Children's information, school materials, schedules
- Alarm panels, keypads, codes, security camera details
- Computer screens, tablets, TVs showing private content
- Vehicle license plates (unless irrelevant/obscured)
- Access codes, lockbox combinations, keys in view
- Anything inside medicine cabinets, bedside drawers, or personal storage (no photographing
  inside personal storage at all)

## Shooting standards for marketing

1. Ask permission first — before the photo, ideally at booking for recurring customers.
2. Frame tight: the detail, the surface, the result — not the whole room with identifying
   context.
3. People: no one appears in a marketing photo without explicit written permission (including
   the founder's own family members).
4. No staged dirt, no re-arranged mess to "prove" a before, no dark-filter tricks.
5. Before/after pairs should be the same angle and honest light.

## Handling customer requests

- A customer can revoke marketing permission at any time. When they do: remove the photo from
  the site/content and from scheduled social posts; log the change.
- If a customer asks for completion photos to be deleted after delivery, honor it unless a
  legal/insurance obligation requires retention (document it if so).

## Unintentional capture

If a photo accidentally includes something on the never-list: delete it — do not blur-and-hope.
If it was already published: remove it, regenerate affected assets, and note the incident.

## Marketing library hygiene

- Keep a simple record per published photo: job reference, permission (yes/date), platforms
  used. If there's no permission record, the photo doesn't get published.
- Sanitize screenshots of checklists/reports before they're used as marketing (no customer
  names, addresses or notes visible).
