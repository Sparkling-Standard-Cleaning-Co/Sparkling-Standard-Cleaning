# Content engine

Content exists to acquire and retain customers — not to fill a calendar. This document defines the
brand device, the content hierarchy, the visual proof engine, the scoring model, the kill rules and
the 90-day command system. It extends `CONTENT-OPERATING-SYSTEM.md` (channel roles, pillars) and
`CONTENT-PRODUCTION-SYSTEM.md` (production workflow and the proposed four-week plan) — it does not
replace them.

## 1. The brand line: "Standards That Make Your Space Sparkle."

The owner-approved slogan is **"Standards That Make Your Space Sparkle."** Use it as the closing anchor for a
piece, then let the real work demonstrate the promise. It supports transformation, process, quality,
consistency, care and customer experience — never price, discounts or urgency.

Rules for the line:

- It is a closing line or an overlay, not a hook. Hooks stop the scroll; the line is the signature.
- Use it once per asset. Do not repeat it mechanically in every caption.

## 2. The content hierarchy (six levels of purpose)

Every asset serves one level. Higher levels are rarer and more valuable; lower levels keep the
engine running.

| Level | Purpose | Typical content | Primary platforms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **L1 — Attention** | Stop the scroll; get discovered | Signature detail demos, satisfying transformations | TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts |
| **L2 — Trust** | Show there is a real standard and a real person | Process proof, founder content, quality-control checklist | Instagram, TikTok, Facebook |
| **L3 — Proof** | Show real results on real homes | Before/after, case studies, real review content | Instagram, GBP, website, Facebook |
| **L4 — Decision** | Answer the questions people ask before hiring a cleaner | What's included, how estimates work, recurring explained, move-out/STR checklists | Website, Instagram, Facebook, Nextdoor, GBP |
| **L5 — Local** | Be findable and near | Neighborhood notes, availability, local relevance | GBP, Facebook, Nextdoor, site |
| **L6 — Retention & advocacy** | Keep customers and multiply them | Recurring value, between-visit tips, review/referral moments | Email/SMS, Instagram stories, Facebook |

If a piece of content does not clearly serve a level, question whether it belongs.

## 3. Content categories (what we make)

1. **Transformation** — real before/after from a permissioned job.
2. **Process proof** — how the cleaning is actually done (the method, the checklist, the order).
3. **Detail proof** — corners, edges, fixtures, surfaces; the signature format.
4. **Customer decision content** — what's included, deep vs standard, first recurring clean,
   estimates, move-out timelines, STR turnover.
5. **Founder / trust** — Hayli, her background (owner-approved facts only), her standards.
6. **Local** — neighborhood/community presence, availability, local windows.
7. **Customer proof** — genuine reviews and recurring relationships, once they exist.
8. **Lifestyle / relief** — what professional cleaning gives back: time, relief, consistency,
   comfort, reduced workload.

The strongest content makes people think: **"They actually pay attention."**

## 4. The visual proof engine — one clean, many assets

One real cleaning job should produce a batch of assets. This is the existing one-asset-many-
publications matrix (`MULTIPLATFORM-OPERATING-PLAN.md` §3) applied to field capture
(`FIELD-CONTENT-CAPTURE.md`):

| From one job (permissioned) | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| Before photo set (wide + detail) | Transformation post, case-study material, future website proof |
| After photo set (same angles) | Before/after reveal, carousel, GBP photo update |
| 15–45 s detail clip(s) | Instagram Reel, TikTok, YouTube Short |
| One short vertical cut | Pinterest image (still), X/Threads/Gab/Parler one-liner (tip) |
| Founder line to camera (optional) | Story, Facebook post, local post |
| Customer review (when it lands) | Story highlight, GBP reply, website reviews (when enough exist) |
| Neighborhood note ("we cleaned on this street") | Nextdoor/Facebook local post, door-knocking relevance |

Rules: real work only. No staged dirt, no fake before/afters, no invented reviews. Never film an
identifiable interior without permission and never publish identifying or private information
(`docs/privacy/PHOTO-PRIVACY-SOP.md`). Do not manufacture content to fill a calendar — real work
generates the best material; when there is no genuine material, post education instead of
fabricating proof.

## 5. Hook library (first two seconds)

Hooks are the whole game on short-form. Rotate and test them; never use the same hook twice in a
row. Examples:

- The detail close-up: "This is the spot nobody cleans."
- The contrast: "Before vs. after — same house, same hour."
- The confession: "I clean houses for a living, and I still can't believe this one."
- The question: "When did you last clean this?"
- The list: "Three places you're forgetting every week."
- The process: "Watch how this takes ten seconds."
- The founder: "I'm 18 and I clean houses better than the companies I worked for." (only with
  owner-approved facts)
- The local: "If you live in [neighborhood], this one's for you." (only real served areas)

Hook rules: show motion or contrast in frame one; one idea per hook; never promise a price, a
discount or instant booking; never claim a result the footage does not show.

## 6. Content scoring model — what to make next

Score every candidate idea 1–5 on each dimension, then apply the weights. Use it when planning the
week; do not over-engineer it.

| Dimension | Weight | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead potential | 25% | Could this make a local homeowner inquire? |
| Recurring potential | 20% | Does it support weekly/biweekly conversion? |
| Visual strength | 15% | Is the footage genuinely satisfying on mute? |
| Trust | 10% | Does it prove a real standard or a real person? |
| Local relevance | 10% | Is it meaningfully local (not generic)? |
| Search value | 5% | Could it rank or answer a real search? |
| Differentiation | 5% | Would a franchise never show this? |
| Production difficulty (inverse) | 5% | Can it be captured in the normal job flow? |
| Reuse potential | 3% | Does one capture feed several platforms/posts? |
| Referral potential | 2% | Would a happy customer share it? |

Weighted score = Σ (rating × weight). Interpretation:

- **≥ 4.0** — make it this week.
- **3.0–3.9** — queue it; make it when the footage naturally exists.
- **< 3.0** — skip it; a lower-value post is not worth the production time.

The score answers "What should we actually make next?" — never "How do we produce the largest
amount of content?"

## 7. Kill rules (when to reduce or stop)

Review at the two-week experiment checkpoint and in the monthly content review. Kill or pause when
a pattern (not one post) shows:

- Consistently low business relevance — views without profile visits, link taps or inquiries.
- High effort / low reuse — bespoke production for a channel that never converts.
- No meaningful traffic or inquiries after a fair test period (4–6 weeks of genuine attempts).
- A channel consumes time without strategic value (repurpose-only channels never get original
  production).
- Vanity metrics rise while downstream actions (inquiries, bookings) stay flat.
- Content requires claims or staging that violate the no-fabrication rule — kill immediately.

Never kill something because one post underperformed. Recut the hook first; a good clip with a bad
hook is a new test, not a failure.

## 8. Recycling and recutting

- A strong clip earns a **new hook and a second life** 3–4 weeks later (different first frame,
  different caption).
- A strong format (e.g., a detail reveal) becomes the **template** for the next captures.
- Monthly: assemble the best-performing assets into one recap/montage; it doubles as a
  proof-of-consistency asset.
- Never repost identical content to the same audience within 30 days; adapt the hook or the cut.
- Photo sets recycle into GBP updates, carousels and future website proof when genuine material is
  sufficient.

## 9. Seasonal marketing (context, not discounts)

Holidays are reasons to talk about preparation, hosting, reset, fresh starts, time and relief —
never automatic discount events. Any promotion requires owner-approved terms and the publication
flag (`docs/launch/PROMOTION-PROPOSALS.md`).

| Window | Context angle | Service tie-in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| October (Halloween) | Hosting, candy-season kitchen reset, porch/entry details | Standard/recurring, entry detail |
| November (Thanksgiving) | Hosting preparation, guest-ready kitchen and bath, time with family | Deep clean, recurring reset |
| December (Christmas/hosting) | Home readiness for guests, calendar overwhelm, giving time back | Deep clean, gift certificates (request mode only until approved) |
| January (New Year) | Fresh start, reset, routine that holds | Recurring plans, deep clean |
| February–March | Early spring reset, windows and light return | Deep clean, recurring |
| March–April (spring cleaning) | The season the category exists for; detail list | Deep clean, first recurring clean |
| May (Mother's Day) | Give time back; relief for the household manager | Recurring plans |
| June–August (summer) | Guests, kids home, humidity and floors; travel season | Recurring, STR turnovers |
| August (back-to-school) | Routines restart, household capacity | Recurring, deep clean |
| September–October (fall reset) | Preparing the home for closed-window season | Deep clean, recurring |
| Year-round (moving periods) | Month-end/lease cycles; summer peak; post-holiday moves | Move-in/move-out, recurring |
| Year-round (hosting/holiday windows) | STR check-in calendars | STR turnovers |

Post seasonal content **before** the window (people plan ahead), not during.

## 10. 90-day content command system

A 12-week operating structure. It is a command system, not a content quota: each week has one
primary acquisition intent, and the content that supports it. The first four weeks implement the
proposed four-week plan in `CONTENT-PRODUCTION-SYSTEM.md`; weeks 5–12 extend it only after the
owner approves and after real footage exists. ~2 core videos + supporting posts per week remains
the sustainable owner-operated pace (`MULTIPLATFORM-OPERATING-PLAN.md` §4).

| Week | Primary intent | Core content (examples) | Supporting posts | CTA / destination |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduce the standard | Founder introduction (long + short); "the detail most people miss" | Local welcome post (Facebook/Nextdoor) | Tracked estimate link |
| 2 | Prove the detail | Faucet base; switch plates; oven glass | Tip post (between-visit care) | Follow the series |
| 3 | Prove the process | "A recurring clean, start to finish"; move-out checklist | Decision post (what's included) | Tracked estimate link |
| 4 | Local + results | Neighborhood availability; month-one recap | Review of the best format | Local tracked link |
| 5 | Recurring value | "First clean vs. maintained"; what stays easier | Recurring FAQ (Nextdoor/Facebook) | Recurring explanation page |
| 6 | Founder trust | "Why I started" second cut; supplies/checklist BTS | Question-answer post | Tracked estimate link |
| 7 | Decision content | Deep vs. standard; what's not included | Comment answer video | Service page link |
| 8 | Local density | "We cleaned on this street" cluster post; neighborhood note | Local proof carousel | Local tracked link |
| 9 | STR/host segment | Turnover walkthrough (host-permissioned) | Host checklist post | STR outreach link |
| 10 | Move-out season | Move-out detail checklist; deposit walkthrough items | Realtor-facing post (professional tone) | Move-out page link |
| 11 | Reviews + referrals | Genuine review content (only if real); "why neighbors refer" | Referral card reminder (no invented incentive) | Review/referral flow |
| 12 | Decide + double down | Recut of the quarter's best performer; format recap | Quarterly review post | Best-performing CTA |

Operating rules:

- Each week publishes from real footage where possible; education fills gaps — never fabrication.
- One master shoot session per week can feed the week's core videos (batch filming,
  `CONTENT-PRODUCTION-SYSTEM.md` §5).
- The weekly scorecard records which posts produced inquiries; the next week's plan responds to it
  (`WEEKLY-SCORECARD.md`).
- If a week collapses: one video + one local/GBP post + one outreach block + one review ask.

## 11. Upgraded 20-post campaign

The proposed four-week plan (16 posts, `CONTENT-PRODUCTION-SYSTEM.md`) remains the starting
campaign. The upgrade adds four high-value posts that connect content to the recurring and referral
engines. Make them when the genuine material exists:

| # | Working title | Category | Why it earns a slot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 17 | "What a recurring clean actually keeps clean" | Recurring value / decision | Speaks directly to the primary business objective (weekly/biweekly customers) |
| 18 | "We're already cleaning on this street" | Local / density | Turns an existing route into social proof for the neighbors; feeds door knocking |
| 19 | "Why neighbors make the best referrals" | Referral education | Sets up the referral moment without inventing incentives |
| 20 | "The detail we fixed because a customer asked" | Trust / make-it-right | Proves the satisfaction policy is real; strongest trust asset a new company can have |

These four are candidates, not obligations — score them with §6 and produce them only from real
material.

## 12. Measurement

Follow the reporting hierarchy in `docs/marketing/LEAD-MEASUREMENT-MODEL.md`; record weekly in
`docs/marketing/WEEKLY-SCORECARD.md`. Content success = inquiries and bookings it contributes to,
not views. Track per asset: platform, hook used, format, tracked-link clicks, inquiries attributed,
and any bookings. The two-week experiment loop (`ACQUISITION-CHANNELS.md` §6) turns that into
decisions.

## 13. Rules that never bend

1. No fabricated proof, reviews, results or claims.
2. Customer property filmed/published only with permission, privacy scrubbed
   (`docs/privacy/PHOTO-PRIVACY-SOP.md`).
3. No price, discount or promotion in content without owner-approved terms and publication flags.
4. Tracked links only from `docs/marketing/WHERE-TO-PASTE-UTM-LINKS.md`; never on internal
   navigation or profile links.
5. The content plan is a hypothesis; the market decides. Kill by pattern, not by mood.
